\begin{abstract}
Open-world test-time adaptation must improve recognition under distribution
shift without allowing unknown samples to corrupt the adaptation process.
STAMP addresses this problem with consistency filtering, entropy-based
admission to a class-balanced memory, and stable replay, but its detector and
adaptation objective both depend on prediction entropy. We study whether
outlier scores imported from out-of-distribution detection can provide a
better admission signal while leaving the remainder of STAMP unchanged. Our
first hypothesis was that distance to clean source features would separate
outliers from shifted in-distribution samples and decouple detection from the
adapting classifier. A K-nearest-neighbor (KNN) variant produced mixed evidence.
On CIFAR-10-C with noise outliers it achieved 78.64\% accuracy but only 59.10\%
AUC and a 63.61\% H-score; on CIFAR-100-C it achieved 45.73\%, 69.93\%, and
54.15\%, respectively. Although KNN--STAMP improved accuracy over the frozen
source model, test-time normalization obtained higher H-scores on both
benchmarks. We hypothesize that severe corruption moves valid samples away
from the clean reference bank, weakening a fixed feature-distance test. We
therefore replace KNN with logit energy and a rolling, label-free threshold,
which require neither source-reference retrieval nor additional model passes.
The energy variant attains 57.14\% accuracy, 95.12\% AUC, and 71.33\% H-score
on CIFAR-100-C, outperforming both baselines; on CIFAR-10-C, it reaches 78.99\%
accuracy but 54.33\% AUC and a 63.80\% H-score. Thus, energy scoring is highly
effective in the 100-class setting but does not consistently improve the joint
recognition--rejection trade-off across datasets. A third, ongoing experiment
uses adaptive conformal inference to calibrate the admission threshold online
while holding the underlying score fixed; its results are reserved to test
whether threshold drift, rather than score choice alone, explains this gap.
\end{abstract}
