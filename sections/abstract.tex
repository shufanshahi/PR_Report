\begin{abstract}
Open-world test-time adaptation must improve recognition under distribution
shift without allowing unknown samples to corrupt the adaptation process.
STAMP addresses this problem with consistency filtering, entropy-based
admission to a class-balanced memory, and stable replay, but its detector and
adaptation objective both depend on prediction entropy. We study whether
outlier scores from out-of-distribution detection can provide a better
admission signal while leaving the remainder of STAMP unchanged. A
nearest-neighbor variant based on clean source features produced mixed results,
as severe corruption can move valid samples away from the reference bank. We
therefore evaluate logit energy with a rolling, label-free threshold, avoiding
source-reference retrieval and additional model passes. Energy scoring improves
outlier detection in the larger benchmark but does not consistently improve the
combined recognition--rejection trade-off. Adaptive conformal inference closely
matches the energy baseline without providing a clear improvement. Finally, we
correct an implementation error in STAMP's replay loss that improperly scaled
gradients when the memory was partially filled. This correction produces the
strongest completed H-scores, showing that accurate replay-loss scaling can
matter as much as the choice of outlier score.
\end{abstract}
