\section{Introduction}

Image classifiers are commonly trained under the assumption that deployment
data follow the training distribution. In practice, image corruptions alter
the input distribution, and an open-world stream can additionally contain
samples from classes that were never observed during training. Test-time
adaptation (TTA) uses unlabeled deployment data to reduce this mismatch, for
example by updating normalization statistics or minimizing prediction
entropy~\cite{schneider2020corruptions,wang2021tent}. The same unlabeled stream,
however, may contain outliers. Updating a model on confidently misclassified
outliers can reinforce its mistakes, so open-world TTA must jointly preserve
known-class recognition and reject unknown inputs.

STAMP was designed for this joint setting~\cite{yu2024stamp}. It averages
predictions over augmented views, applies consistency and entropy filters,
stores accepted samples in a class-balanced memory, and adapts normalization
parameters through self-weighted entropy minimization with stable memory
replay. This design improves the reliability of the update set, but prediction
entropy has two responsibilities: it decides which samples may influence
adaptation and it supplies the adaptation objective. A fixed, dataset-specific
entropy cutoff must also remain suitable while the model and test distribution
change. These observations led us to ask a narrow question: can the outlier
detector be replaced by a signal from an adjacent field while the memory and
optimization mechanisms of STAMP remain fixed?

We investigate this question as a sequence of falsifiable interventions rather
than assuming that a plausible replacement must improve performance. Our first
hypothesis was that non-parametric KNN distance in the penultimate feature space
would be more sensitive to unknown inputs than output entropy and less coupled
to the model's changing confidence. We therefore cached normalized source
features and used mean distance to the nearest references as the memory-admission
and OOD score. The result was mixed. Relative to the frozen source model, the
variant substantially improved known-class accuracy on both CIFAR-10-C and
CIFAR-100-C with noise outliers. Relative to test-time normalization, however,
it reduced AUC by 9.41 and 10.98 percentage points and reduced H-score by 6.97
and 4.34 points, respectively. Thus, the experiment did not support the central
expectation that source-feature distance would improve the joint recognition
and rejection trade-off.

Our working explanation is that the clean reference bank is itself the wrong
anchor under severe corruption: the corruption moves in-distribution features
as well as outlier features, so distance can reject useful samples and starve
the memory of adaptation data. This is a diagnosis to be tested, not a
conclusion inferred from aggregate metrics alone. It motivates our second
hypothesis: free energy computed from the logits can improve admission without
depending on a fixed source bank~\cite{liu2020energy}. The energy variant
strongly supports this hypothesis on CIFAR-100-C, improving the H-score from
54.15 for KNN--STAMP and 58.49 for test-time normalization to 71.33. However,
its CIFAR-10-C AUC falls to 54.33, below both KNN--STAMP and normalization.
This contrast shows that eliminating a stale feature bank is helpful but is not
by itself a dataset-independent solution. The implementation records the
rejection rate of ground-truth in-distribution samples and the admission rate
of outliers for post-hoc diagnosis; these measurements are needed to explain
the remaining CIFAR-10-C failure mode.

The contrasting energy results also expose a limitation shared by the first two
rounds: both ultimately convert a score into a hard admission decision using a
threshold. Replacing the score does not by itself guarantee that this cutoff
remains calibrated as the stream changes. Our third, ongoing intervention
therefore imports adaptive conformal inference (ACI) from distribution-free
statistics~\cite{gibbs2021aci}. ACI adjusts the score quantile online to target
the desired rejection rate. We run it with the original entropy score to isolate
threshold calibration, and with energy to test the combined intervention. The
results are intentionally left pending.

Finally, we found a separate implementation-level issue in the replay loss.
STAMP uses confidence-weighted entropy over a memory of capacity 64, but the
memory is repeatedly reset and initially contains fewer than 64 accepted
samples. The prior implementation normalized the weights for capacity 64 and
then averaged over the actual replay size, unintentionally multiplying the loss
and descent gradient by $64/N$ when $N<64$. Our fourth intervention replaces
this capacity constant with the actual replay size. Unlike the preceding score
and threshold experiments, this is a correctness fix: its purpose is to make
the intended confidence-weighted average independent of memory occupancy.
It yields the best completed H-scores on both benchmarks, including 81.19 on
CIFAR-10-C and 72.89 on CIFAR-100-C.

The contributions of this work are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a controlled study of outlier scoring in STAMP that changes the
    admission signal while preserving its consistency filter, balanced memory,
    and replay objective;
    \item evidence that KNN feature distance can improve classification yet
    worsen the joint accuracy--detection trade-off under severe corruption; and
    \item an energy-based follow-up with online thresholding that substantially
    improves the joint metric on CIFAR-100-C, while revealing a remaining
    dataset-dependent detection failure on CIFAR-10-C; and
    \item an ongoing ACI threshold-calibration study that distinguishes the
    effect of adaptive thresholding from the choice of entropy or energy score;
    and
    \item a batch-size-invariant replay-loss correction that removes a $64/N$
    warm-up scaling error and produces the best completed joint results.
\end{itemize}
